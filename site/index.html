Read this first

Blindspot measures the visibility of a problem in one official database. It does not measure how well a country is doing.

Indicator series

One statistic tracked over time

For example, maternal mortality or access to electricity. Blindspot selects three series for each goal to create a focused, balanced first release.

Global goal

One of the UN's 17 SDGs

The Sustainable Development Goals organize international targets on poverty, health, climate, institutions, and other shared priorities.

Measurement gap

Evidence that is absent or late

A gap means a matching observation is not visible in the UN source when expected. It does not prove that a country collected no data.

04

How it works—and what comes next

The operational atlas is complete as a v0.1. The broader research program is not.

Absence ≠ failure

“Not observed” means the selected official source has no matching country–series–year observation. It does not prove a country failed to collect data.

Status is preserved

Country, adjusted, estimated, modeled, and global-monitoring observations remain distinguishable rather than being silently collapsed.

Applicability first

Conditional indicators are visible for exploration but excluded from priority scores until a defensible country-level applicability registry exists.

Prediction is experimental

The continuity model is evaluated through temporal backtests and yields to a simpler baseline when it does not improve calibration.

Default priority formula

100 × (35% staleness + 25% missingness + 20% global scarcity + 20% population percentile)

Every component is scaled from 0 to 1. Population uses a percentile of log population so the largest countries do not dominate linearly.

Full measurement dictionary
Recent completeness
Distinct observed years divided by the number expected during the latest five completed years, based on a manually curated cadence.
Staleness
Expected reporting periods since the latest observation, scaled from 0 to 100 and capped after three overdue periods.
Regularity
Consistency of gaps between reporting years; calculated only when at least three observed years exist.
Disaggregation
Whether more than one non-total category is visible for sex, age, or location. Observation status—country, adjusted, estimated, modeled, or global monitoring—is preserved separately.
Revision history
Compact snapshots and cell-level deltas beginning with Blindspot's first retrieval; earlier publication vintages cannot be reconstructed.
Preregistered next analysis
  1. Peer-check the frozen source snapshot and checksum.
  2. Compare completeness distributions by income group, region, and goal.
  3. Fit a country-clustered descriptive model using only the declared covariates.
  4. Report effect sizes, uncertainty, missing context, and sensitivity to modeled observations, population weighting, and conditional indicators.

This analysis is observational: associations cannot identify why an observation is absent.

Reproducible observatory

Official-source ingestion, 9,843 country–series assessments, interactive scores, temporal model evaluation, revision tracking, downloads, weekly refreshes, and validation.

Next

Peer-check and inequality analysis

Review the first frozen snapshot, then test whether reporting gaps are associated with region, income group, population, and indicator family—with uncertainty and sensitivity analyses.

Later

Broader evidence system

Add defensible country-level applicability, deeper disaggregation, more indicators and national sources, and enough publication vintages to study revisions over time.

Novelty boundary

A distinctive synthesis, not a claim to be first

Other projects assess statistical capacity, openness, and SDG outcomes. Blindspot's contribution is the continuously reproducible country–series–year layer: source status, coverage, revision deltas, challengeable priority scores, and downloadable evidence behind each public view.

Research question

Who is visible in global development data?

The preregistered next analysis asks whether recent reporting gaps vary with income group, region, population, and indicator family. It is observational and cannot identify why data are absent.
